\## Abstract

Ecotones between freshwater bodies and terrestrial vegetation form dynamic biological tension zones characterized by sharp structural transitions and cross-boundary resource fluxes. Between January 2025 and early November 2026, we conducted systematic multi-site avifaunal surveys across five distinct ecotonal habitats in Odisha: (1) scrub/grassland margin, (2) lacustrine macrophyte fringe, (3) emergent marsh–deciduous canopy interface, (4) littoral woodland buffer, and (5) riparian riverbed sandbars. A total of 1,557 individual detections representing 77 species across 39 families were documented across standardized field checklists. Community diversity varied with vegetation structure: equitability was highest in open scrub margins (CL1: J' = 0.912, H' = 3.670), whereas aggregate abundance peaked in riverine floodplain corridors (CL5: N = 494, S = 60) due to colonial foraging aggregations of Anastomus oscitans. Pairwise Bray-Curtis dissimilarity (BC ranging from 0.491 to 0.751) and Hellinger-transformed Principal Component Analysis (accounting for 73.6% of variance across PC1 and PC2) delineated clear compositional turnover between open alluvial habitats and closed woody canopies. A curated library of 11 verifiable photographic vouchers substantiates vertical niche partitioning across six structural strata, active breeding recruitment (Porphyrio poliocephalus tending downy chicks on floating macrophyte platforms), and cross-boundary trophic subsidization (amphibian predation by Halcyon smyrnensis). Furthermore, the surveys verified the persistence of globally threatened riverine taxa (Sterna acuticauda, Endangered; Sterna aurantia, Vulnerable). These findings emphasize that conserving wetland basins in isolation without protecting contiguous woody terrestrial buffer corridors impairs critical foraging lookouts and breeding micro-refugia across eastern India.
